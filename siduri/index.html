At the tavern by the cosmic sea

The counsel.

At the edge of the sea, the alewife answers the impossible life Gilgamesh seeks with the mortal life already within reach.

O Gilgamesh, where are you wandering?

The life that you seek you never will find:

when the gods created mankind,

death they dispensed to mankind,

life they kept for themselves.

But you, Gilgamesh, let your belly be full,

enjoy yourself always by day and by night!

Make merry each day,

dance and play day and night!

Let your clothes be clean,

let your head be washed, may you bathe in water!

Gaze on the child who holds your hand,

let your wife enjoy your repeated embrace!

For such is the destiny of mortal men.

Translation

Andrew George, The Epic of Gilgamesh (1999)

After the passage

The mortal measure.

The message of the Gilgamesh epic is the vanity of the hero's quest. Pursuit of immortality is folly. The proper duty of men is to accept the mortal life that is his lot and enjoy it to the full.

— Grant L. Voth, “The Epic of Gilgamesh”

Textual note

A parallel beside Tablet X.

This counsel survives on an Old Babylonian tablet assembled from fragments associated with Sippar. It parallels Siduri’s encounter with Gilgamesh in the Standard Babylonian Tablet X, but the familiar “make merry” speech is not preserved there in this form.

The distinction matters: the scene belongs to the epic’s long textual history, while this particular counsel reaches us through an older witness.
